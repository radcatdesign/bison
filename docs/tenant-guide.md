# Bison Ops: a guide for your team

Bison Ops shows you who visits your website, collects everyone who contacts you, and, when you add them, helps you sell and email from the same place.

## Getting around

There are three modes along the top, or along the bottom on your phone:

- **Analyze** shows your visitors and your contacts. Everyone on your team can see it.
- **Marketing** is for email and pages: newsletters, automatic email series, your subscriber list, landing pages and how you show up in search.
- **Sales** is for estimates, invoices, orders and your online store.

Bison Ops opens the mode you used last. **Updates** lists every change we make. **Settings** holds your plan, your team and your connections.

On your phone, tap **Menu** in the bar at the bottom for the pages inside a mode. Pull down from the top of any page to refresh it. To put Bison Ops on your home screen, open it in your phone's browser and choose Add to Home Screen.

## Fit Funnels, for clubs

Gyms, health clubs and fitness studios use Bison Ops as **Fit Funnels**: the same tools, with your club's words. If you signed up as a club, everything below works the same way, with these names:

- **Leads** are the people who asked about your club: a free trial pass, a tour, a class or a personal training consult. **Members** are the ones who joined.
- **Funnel pages** are landing pages, with templates for a free trial pass, an intro offer, a new class, a personal training consult, a presale, corporate wellness, a six-week challenge and a club tour.
- **Intro offer** is the welcome offer: a one-time code for each new subscriber, like a first week free. Your numbers, never ours.
- **Memberships** are what you sell: memberships, class packs, personal training and gear. **Purchases** are paid orders, **Quotes** are for corporate memberships, team events and training bundles, **Corporate accounts** are companies buying for their staff at corporate pricing, **Promo codes** are discount codes, and **Unfinished joins** are carts people left behind.

Anything only you know, like prices, class times and member numbers, is marked in yellow until you fill it in. Nothing goes out with a yellow spot left.

### The front desk kiosk

Any tablet with a browser can be your front desk. In Settings, Kiosk:

1. Set a staff PIN. Staff type it on the tablet to leave the kiosk.
2. Choose Pair a tablet. On the tablet, open the kiosk address shown and type the 6-digit code within 10 minutes.
3. Add the page to the tablet's home screen so it opens full screen, and turn on the tablet's guided access or kiosk mode.
4. Pick what shows: check in, a free pass, a tour, joining, the waiver. Write your waiver (start from ours, then have a lawyer read it) before turning it on.

People who claim a pass or book a tour land in Leads. Check-ins, passes, tours and waivers show in Analyze, Front desk, and on each lead. Joining shows a code they scan to pay on their own phone. Unpair a lost tablet from Settings and it stops working right away.

## Sample data

A new account starts with sample data, so every screen shows how it works before your own visitors arrive. Sample data is made up, and nothing you change while it's on is saved. When your own data starts coming in, turn it off in **Settings**, under Data. Only owners and admins can switch it.

In Marketing, sample data covers the people: subscribers, welcome codes and results. Your newsletters, series, brand kit and settings save either way, so you can set everything up first. Sending to your list waits until sample data is off, because the list on screen is made up. Tests to yourself work any time.

In Sales, sample data covers orders, estimates, carts and trade accounts. Your products, discount codes and settings save either way, and your shop works for real, so you can set it all up and place a test order first.

## What the numbers mean

Every number has a **?** next to it. Tap it to see exactly how it's counted. A few worth knowing:

- **Visitors** count each browser once per day. We don't use cookies to do it, and nobody can be followed from one day to the next.
- **Average active visit** only counts time when someone is really there: the page is on screen and they've scrolled, clicked, typed or moved in the last two minutes.
- **Bounce rate** is the share of visits that saw one page, did nothing, and left within 10 seconds.

## Adding modules

If your plan doesn't include a mode, opening it shows what it does and an **Unlock** button. From there, an owner can start a 14-day trial with no card, or subscribe. When a module is turned off, your data stays. Turn it back on and everything is where you left it.

## Setting up Marketing

Open **Marketing**, then **Setup**. It lists every step for every tool in the order that works best, ticks each one off as soon as it's really done, and points to the next one. Each tool also shows its own steps at the top of its page until they're finished; fold them away any time. Plan on about an hour, most of it waiting for DNS.

### 1. Email sending (Settings, Email sending)

An owner or admin does this once. It lets your email come from your own address, which is what gets it into inboxes.

1. **Add your domain.** Use a subdomain like `mail.yourcompany.com`. Your everyday email keeps working exactly as it does, and marketing email can't hurt its reputation.
2. **Add the DNS records.** Bison Ops shows three records with copy buttons. Add them wherever your domain's DNS is managed (the page has steps for Cloudflare, GoDaddy, Namecheap and Squarespace, and a note for everyone else). If someone else handles your DNS, send them the three records. Leave the page open and it checks every 20 seconds. Most hosts take a few minutes; some take up to a day.
3. **Pick your From name and address,** like "Ridgeline Roasters" and hello@mail.yourcompany.com, and where replies go.
4. **Send yourself a test.** It lands in your own inbox, from your own address.

You never need an API key or a password for any of this.

### 2. Brand kit

Your logo, colors, fonts, button shape, sign-off and social links, set once. Every email and new landing page uses them. **Your postal address is required** (US law, and similar rules elsewhere); a PO box is fine. Nothing sends until it's in.

### 3. Subscribers

- **"I am a..."** Signup forms ask what kind of customer someone is. You start with a few choices for your industry; rename, add or remove them. Renaming keeps everyone who picked it.
- **Import** a CSV exported from Mailchimp, Constant Contact or a spreadsheet. It needs an Email column; name and type columns are picked up too. You'll confirm everyone agreed to get email from you. People already on your list only get blanks filled in, and anyone who unsubscribed before stays unsubscribed.
- **Lists** are optional. Make them if you send different things to different groups.
- **Signup forms.** Open any landing page, edit its form and turn on "Add to email list". The product launch and free guide templates come with it on.

Everyone who unsubscribes, bounces or marks an email as spam goes on a do-not-email list that every send checks. Your team can't put them back; they can sign up again themselves.

### 4. Welcome series

Three welcome emails are written for you. Fill in the yellow spots (only you know what you're known for), then turn the series on. Everyone who joins gets the first email right away and the rest on schedule. You can add series for other moments too: someone sending a particular form, or one you start yourself for a list. Turning a series off pauses it; turned back on, everyone picks up where they were, without getting several at once.

### 5. Newsletters

Pick a template or start blank, then click any part of the email to change it. The panel on the right edits whatever you clicked; **Email** holds the subject line and preview text. Yellow spots mark things only you know, and sending waits until they're filled in. Send yourself a test, then send it to everyone or one list, now or up to 30 days ahead. Results show how many were sent, delivered, bounced and unsubscribed.

### 6. Welcome offer (optional)

A one-time code for each new subscriber's first order: a percent or amount off, how many days it lasts, and any fine print. Add a "Welcome offer code" section to your first welcome email and turn the offer on; each person sees their own code. With Sales, your shop checks codes at checkout on its own (first order only). Without it, look a code up on this page when someone uses it and mark it used.

### 7. Search pack

Describe your business in a plain paragraph and add your phone, email and hours. Bison Ops turns that and your published pages into a sitemap, `llms.txt` for AI assistants, titles, social cards and structured data. Then paste your sitemap address into Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools and mark that step done; it's the only step nobody can check for you. You can also keep AI crawlers out, and copy lines for your own website's robots.txt.

## Building a landing page

Pages are in Marketing. Describe the page you want in plain words: who it's for, what you're offering, and what people should do. For example: "A spring sale page for glow aggregate, 15% off through May, with a quote form and a few common questions." The agent builds it section by section while you watch.

- **Or start from a template.** Pick one of eight, from a quote request to a free guide. It opens with your company name and brand color already in; untick "Use my brand color" to keep the template's own colors.
- **Change it by asking.** "Make it dark", "add an FAQ", "shorten the copy". Undo takes back any change.
- **Or change it by hand.** Open Sections to edit any text, reorder sections or add new ones. Page sets the colors, type and corners.
- **Check the yellow.** The agent never makes up prices, dates, reviews or numbers. Anything only you know is marked in yellow, and the page can't go live until those are filled in.
- **Publish.** The page goes live at its own address. Anyone who sends its form shows up in Contacts.

Desktop, tablet and phone buttons show the page at each size. On a phone, Preview is its own tab.

## Testing a page (A/B tests)

A test shows some visitors your page as it is (A) and some a changed version (B), then tells you which one gets more people to act. Open **Marketing**, then **A/B tests**. The steps at the top tick off as you go.

1. **Publish the page first.** A test needs a live page to compare against, and a few hundred visitors to learn anything, so pick one that already gets traffic.
2. **Start a test.** Press **New test**, pick the page, name the test, and choose what counts as a win: someone sending the form, or someone clicking any button. Write down what you expect, if you like. It keeps everyone honest later.
3. **Change B.** B starts as a copy of the live page. Press **Edit B** and change it in the usual editor, by hand or by asking the agent. Change one thing, like the headline, the offer or the button, so you know what made the difference. Press **Done** to go back. Want to try two ideas? Add a C.
4. **Check the split.** Half see A and half see B unless you change it. Each version needs at least 10%.
5. **Start it.** The checklist says what's left. Once it starts, the versions and the split are locked, and the page can't be published until the test ends.
6. **Read the results.** The test page says in a sentence where things stand. Each version shows its visitors, its rate, how it compares with A and the chance it's really better. Give it at least a week and 100 visitors on each version; nothing is called before then, and a version has to stay clearly ahead for 3 days in a row before it's called the winner, so a lucky streak can't decide it.
7. **Keep the winner.** Press **End the test** and pick the version to keep. If it's B or C, it goes live right away. Or tick "End on its own" and it ends itself once one version is clearly ahead.

Each visitor sees the same version every time, and no cookie is set. Bots, link previews and anyone signed in to Bison Ops in that browser aren't counted. Your team can still look at the page; they just don't move the numbers. Starting and ending a test needs a role that can publish pages.

## Setting up Sales

Open **Sales**, then **Setup**. Like Marketing, it lists every step in order, ticks each off when it's really done, and each tool shows its own steps at the top of its page. Plan on about an hour, most of it adding products.

### 1. Payments (Settings, Payments)

An owner or admin connects your company's own Stripe account. Every payment from your shop and every invoice goes straight to it; Bison Ops never holds your money and takes no cut.

1. **Connect Stripe.** Bison Ops sends you to Stripe to make an account or use the one you have. Stripe asks for your business details and bank account. You never paste a key.
2. **Check it's live.** Back in Bison Ops, it shows whether Stripe can take payments yet, or what Stripe still needs from you.
3. **Sales tax (optional).** Turn on Stripe Tax in your Stripe Dashboard and add where you're registered to collect, then turn on tax in Sales, Checkout.

### 2. Products

Import them or add them one at a time.

- **Import** a spreadsheet saved as CSV (Name and Price, plus anything else you have), a Shopify export, your Shopify store's address, or the product feed you give Google. Rows with the same name become one product with options. Importing again updates what you imported before.
- **Options** like Size and Color make a version for each combination, each with its own price, was price (for a sale), SKU and weight.
- **Weights and boxes** let checkout charge real shipping. Set what each version weighs, which box it goes in and how many fit. Mark anything that ships in its own box, or give it its own flat shipping charge.
- **Photos.** Upload them or paste image addresses. The first one is what people see in your shop and when it's shared.
- **Active** products show in your shop and each has its own page, with share buttons and the details search engines read.

### 3. Shipping (Settings, Shipping)

Add where you ship from, check your boxes, and choose how to charge: live rates from **ShipStation** or **UPS**, a flat rate, or free. You can add a handling charge and free shipping over an amount. Connect ShipStation and paid orders go there to be packed and labeled. Carrier keys are saved encrypted and can't be read back, only replaced.

### 4. Checkout (Sales, Checkout)

Who hears about each order (owners and admins until you change it), the countries you ship to, a line under the Pay button like your returns policy, and, if you'd rather quote big orders yourself, an order limit. Above it, "Check out" becomes "Ask for a quote", and the cart lands in Estimates as a draft.

### 5. Your first order

Open your shop (the **Your shop** button on the Sales overview), add something to the cart and check out. Every paid order is in **Orders**, with the payment in Stripe, where it is in ShipStation, the team email, and refunds.

### 6. Estimates

Start one, add lines from your catalog or type them in, then send it. Your client gets an email with a link to their own page, where they approve it by typing their name, ask for changes, or decline. When they approve, the invoice goes out with a Pay link. When they pay, it's an order. If they pay by check, mark it paid. Every step shows in the estimate's history.

### 7. Discount codes (optional)

Percent or amount off, for anyone or one trade account, once per buyer, once ever or unlimited, with a total limit, a minimum order and dates. Each code is spelled out in a sentence before you save it. Codes are private: they only work when someone types one. A code that takes the whole order off ships it free with no payment.

### 8. Trade accounts (optional)

Share your trade page (Sales, Trade accounts has the address) with builders, designers and resellers. When you approve someone, they get an email with a link that signs them in, no password, and they see their own prices across the shop. Tiers give better prices as they buy more over 12 months, or you can set someone's tier yourself. Pausing an account signs them out everywhere.

### Carts and following up

**Carts** shows everyone who gave their email at checkout, with left-behind carts first. With Marketing, make an email series that starts when "Someone leaves a cart" to win them back.

### Sharing a contact

Open a contact in **Contacts** and make a share link to send someone outside your team, like an installer. It shows who they are, what they sent and what they've bought, for 1, 7 or 30 days, and you can turn it off any time.

## Your team

| Role | Sees | Can |
|---|---|---|
| Owner | Everything | Everything, including the plan, email sending, Stripe, shipping and deleting the account |
| Admin | Everything | Everything except the plan, including email sending, Stripe and shipping |
| Sales | Analyze and Sales | Work contacts, send email, run estimates, orders, products and codes, issue refunds, share contacts |
| Marketing | Analyze and Marketing | Work contacts, build and send newsletters and series, manage subscribers, build and publish landing pages |
| Viewer | Analyze | Look only |

## Your data

Your company's data is kept apart from every other company's by the database itself, not just by our code. You can export everything, or delete any one person's data by their email address. Those tools arrive with the next release. Your subscriber list can be exported as a CSV from Subscribers any time.
